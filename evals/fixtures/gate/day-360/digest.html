US requires export licences for AI chips shipped to five countries, to cut China's remote access

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued a rule on Monday that requires licences for advanced chip shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil. It says the five carry a higher-than-normal risk of diversion to China. Chinese firms barred from buying top GPUs had been able to rent them legally at data centres in those countries. The rule takes effect 90 days after it is posted to the Federal Register. Singapore said it is studying the measure, and Asian stocks fell.

Why it matters

The rule targets the loophole that Megaspeed, the Singapore-based firm at the centre of the Nvidia scandal, is accused of exploiting. It lands as Trump heads to a summit with Xi, and it puts Southeast Asia's data centre boom in the line of a US-China fight.
