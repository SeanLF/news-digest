UN says 25 seafarers killed near Hormuz since February as Iran reports three more tankers hit mines

At least 51 seafarers have been killed in and around the Strait of Hormuz since the US-Israeli war on Iran began in February, UN official Khaled Khiari told the Security Council on Friday. Iranian authorities said on Saturday that three oil tankers hit mines in "unauthorised" or "restricted" areas of the strait and caught fire. Iran's Revolutionary Guards earlier said two tankers hit mines. The US military says it has cleared the main routes and the strait is open, but UKMTO puts traffic about 65 percent below pre-war levels.

Why it matters

Iran says the strait stays closed until the US ends its naval blockade and meets other demands, while Washington says the lanes are clear. Ships caught between those two claims are now the ones hitting mines, and the UN says the cost is passing to energy, insurance and fertiliser markets.

Turkey and Qatar sign mutual defence pact, pledging a unified stance against any attack

Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defence agreement in Istanbul on Friday, after President Erdogan met Qatar's emir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani. The deal commits them to a common position against any external armed attack on either country. It also covers consultations on threats, joint exercises, defence-industry cooperation and personnel training. Reported communique text did not say what would trigger the pact or what a joint response would involve; analysts told Al Jazeera it aims to deter attacks, not build an offensive alliance. It follows an August Turkey-Pakistan-Saudi Arabia defence pact, ratified by Turkey's parliament last week.
